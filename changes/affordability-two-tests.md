### Changed — the housing report answers "which era was harder" on two tests

- The headline no longer rests on first-year principal and interest as a
  share of income, which flattered the present. The report now asks two
  questions: can you get in (cash to close as a share of a year's income),
  and can you carry it (the full payment with property tax and insurance,
  per household and per single full-time earner).
- Each year's buyer is followed for up to ten years, refinancing when rates
  fall a full point, so a temporary high rate and a lasting high price no
  longer look alike. A chart shows each buyer's payment path.
- Prices are existing homes (FHFA repeat-sales index anchored to NAR's
  median) instead of the Census median of houses sold, which is mostly new
  construction. Income comes straight from Census table H-5; nothing is
  typed in by hand.
- The comparison is now the worst year of the eighties (the 1981 rate
  peak) against the latest full year, in place of 1985 against 2023. The calculator adds closing costs, property
  tax and insurance. A "What this leaves out" list names the limits:
  national medians, flat tax and insurance rates, no projection.
- One number up top: each buyer's cash to close plus ten years of payments,
  as a share of the income earned over those ten years, for every buyer
  since 1979. Buyers whose ten years run past the data get the missing years
  projected on one stated assumption (rates hold at the latest average;
  income and home values grow at income's last-decade pace) and are marked
  projected on the chart and cards. A box under it lets the reader change
  those assumptions; it starts from the same ones, so it matches the chart.
- Simpler page: the one number, then why (the worst of the eighties against
  today, and each buyer's payment path). The rate and price charts, the
  tables and the two year-pickers are gone.
