# UX and UI audit, pass 2 — 2026-10-18

**Status: findings and a short round of fixes.** Taken from main at `306ea21`
after all four steps of [[ux-plan-2026-10-17]] merged: fresh UI build, the
reference project rebuilt, 94 captures of every place, tab, panel and report
at 1440 and 390, light and dark. Zero console errors in any context. The
first audit is [[ux-audit-2026-10-17]]; this one asks what a reader meets now.

The short version: **the structure is right and the system holds.** The four
places read as four places; the reports read as one family in both modes;
trust has one vocabulary. What is left is a layer of *words and
duplication* that the packages, each owning one piece, could not see across
pieces: text written for the agent that the reader now meets first, the same
fact shown twice within one screen, and a handful of finish items.

## Findings

Severity as before: **S1** breaks or misleads the reader's job, **S2**
confuses or wastes, **S3** polish.

### Words a reader meets

1. **S1 — Report descriptions are implementation notes.** The library's
   second line, the first thing a reader scans, reads "The direct-lending
   book as an editorial report: the headline is the finding, every number
   in the prose is a bound figure, and the page carries its own house CSS…"
   or "an app (app.jsx) whose every number is a kit figure, so the receipt
   names it. Edit app.jsx to reshape the page". These are `report.json`
   `description`s written when the reader was an agent. Every reference
   report, both template bundles and the demo app need a description a
   reader would use ("Fair value, unrealized and mark across three funds,
   by sector, fund and issuer"), one sentence. The same text shows in the
   About-this-build panel's first line.
2. **S1 — `portfolio_dashboard`'s masthead says "Portfolio book".** The
   library row says Portfolio Dashboard, the app header says Portfolio
   Dashboard, and the page inside says "Portfolio book" with the kicker
   "Direct Lending". A leftover from the spec conversion. Mastheads should
   carry the report's `name` or a finding, never another report's name.
3. **S2 — The footer line leaks identifiers and tooling.** "Built by
   workflow on 2026-10-18 from the model portfolio_model" / "Built by
   tracebi init --template saas-metrics on 2026-10-18 from the model
   saas_model". A reader wants "Built 18 Oct 2026 · Direct Lending
   Portfolio". The author field is tooling provenance: keep it in the
   receipt's About-this-build, not on the page.
4. **S2 — Kickers are inconsistent.** "DIRECT LENDING", "DIRECT LENDING ·
   PORTFOLIO OVERVIEW", "SAAS METRICS", "SAAS METRICS · COHORT BRIEF". One
   rule: the kicker is the model's label; the h1 is the report's name or
   finding.
5. **S3 — Identifiers still surface** where a label exists: the Map's nodes
   (`housing_model`, `affordability_transform`), the Transform tab's card
   title (`portfolio_model`), the Model tab's grain line
   (`dim_fund.fund, dim_issuer.issuer, dim_issuer.sector`), the footer's
   model name. The sidebar tagline "Analytics trust layer" remains.

### The same fact twice

6. **S2 — Built time and verdict appear twice within 150px** on the report
   page: the app header ("Reproduces · Built Oct 18 · 20:25 · Data refreshed
   Oct 17 · 23:47") and, inside the frame, the masthead meta ("Built Oct 18,
   2026, 8:25 PM · Reproduces · Receipt"). When the app hosts the artifact,
   the app header is the chrome; the masthead's meta should reduce to the
   Receipt link (the host already sends a message; send "hosted" with it).
   Outside the app the masthead meta stays whole.
7. **S2 — The Data page stacks three tab rows.** Page tabs (Model ·
   Transform · Sources · Runs), then inside Model a second row (Diagram ·
   Tables · Measures · Relationships · Storage · Code) with an "Edit as a
   draft" button floating alone above an empty band, plus a badge
   "1 FACT · 2 DIMENSIONS · 13 MEASURES" repeating what the line above
   already says. On a phone the inner row wraps to two lines and the ERD is
   a thumbnail. (Backlog 23, now seen in context.)
8. **S2 — History is a wall of "Not checked".** 40 rows of the same nine
   reports, one per rebuild, and on a phone a 4,000px card list. Older
   builds of the same report carry no information a reader needs by
   default. Group by target: the newest build per report, with "N older
   builds" expanding. The Took and Who columns show as dashes on every
   desktop row because a few CLI rows carry a value; a dash should be
   quieter than text.
9. **S3 — The report page's History tab** has the same wall (20 rows of
   "Build · Not checked"); the same grouping fixes it.

### Report finish

10. **S1 — `affordability`'s Receipt link is invisible**: accent blue on
    the green hero (the masthead meta's link colour is not the hero's ink).
    The chip beside it reads fine. One rule: links inside `.tb-masthead`
    on a hero ground inherit the hero ink.
11. **S2 — Tables cut their last column with no hint.** `mrr_dashboard`'s
    "Ending MRR by plan" shows "LO…" for Logo churn at 1440; every phone
    table clips its right columns inside the card. The scroll works, but
    nothing says so. A right-edge fade while there is more to scroll, on
    `.tb-scroll` and the kit's `<Table>`.
12. **S2 — The showcase's lead KPI reads "285.9M"** with no currency while
    every other page says "$285.9M"; its fourth KPI "Est. Q3 deployments ·
    4" is a `report.py` demonstration that reads as a fact. Format the
    lead as currency; label the python-derived card as such in its context
    line ("from report.py, not re-run"). Its scatter card is 70% empty
    space at 1440.
13. **S3 — Transform tab words.** Flow nodes say `transform` / `build` in
    mono with "3 rows" / "6 rows" (rows of what?); the table-check chips
    are all-caps "CHECKS PASSED"; the Steps table on a phone clips its
    status column. Say "Transform · ran in 2 s · wrote 3 tables", "Build ·
    6 reports"; chips in sentence case as the Verdict chip is.
14. **S3 — Library rows truncate descriptions to one line at 1440** with
    "…" while the phone wraps to two; let the desktop wrap to two as well
    once descriptions are reader-length (finding 1).

### What is right and should not move

The library as home with a chip per row; the four-place sidebar; the
report page as the report with one bar and one panel; the receipt as a
certificate in both the file and the app; the editorial register (the
cohort brief and the book read as letters); charts with direct labels and
no truncation; dark mode as its own palette everywhere; Ask's answer card
and its honesty line; Review's three sections; the Get started page's new
"How the app fits together".

## Round 2 — the fixes

Three packages, small, each with the usual gate and captures:

- **A2-1 Words** (report files + `template_package.py`): reader descriptions
  for every reference, template and demo report; `portfolio_dashboard`'s
  masthead; the kicker rule (model label) and the h1 rule applied across the
  reference project, scaffolds and guides; the footer line as "Built <date> ·
  <model label>" with author/tooling moved to the receipt's About-this-build;
  `AGENTS.md` / `init_agents.md` / `capabilities.py` teach "description is
  for the reader".
- **A2-2 Report finish** (`tracebi.css`, `tracebi.js`, kit, frame bridge,
  two reports): hosted mastheads reduce their meta to the Receipt link;
  masthead links inherit hero ink; the scroll fade on overflowing tables;
  the showcase's currency, python-derived label and scatter height;
  `mrr_dashboard`'s plan table.
- **A2-3 App** (`web/ui`): the Model tab's inner row becomes a segmented
  control in the card header with Edit as a draft beside it, the duplicate
  badge goes, grain in labels; the Transform tab's words and the phone
  Steps table; History grouped by target with older builds folded (page
  and panel), quiet dashes; the Map and Transform card by label; library
  descriptions on two lines; the tagline.

Not in this round, still in [[ui-backlog]]: build durations (the run store
stores no finish time), Ask fingerprints, the two-step Flow zoom.
